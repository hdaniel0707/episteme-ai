Bottom line. All three reviewers accept the core empirical observation (stated probabilities tie at the 1%-FPR threshold; the token-probability readout breaks those ties). The attacks are on (1) whether the comparison isolates the readout: different prompt, 2-decimal output, separate call; (2) oracle thresholds and evaluation-set backbone selection: no deployment-level FPR evidence, and the domain-shift experiment is "flat by construction"; (3) FusionJudge's equal-weight average as unmotivated; and (4) robustness: residual mass / top-k, repeated calls at the real threshold, MG-AI-Sci's one-document threshold. Most of (2)–(4) can be answered on a CPU from data we already have. (1) needs new API runs, and both critical reviewers name it first. WpPs is the reviewer we can most likely move; their list is concrete and they promise to reconsider. Lma8 should be kept and moved up. xghj will be hard to move, but the AC reads that thread, so it has to be answered carefully.

Deadline & how ICLR rebuttals work

We have 8 days, not 10. ICLR 2027 author–reviewer discussion: Nov 5 → Nov 18, 2026 (AoE). AoE Nov 18 23:59 = Thu Nov 19, 12:59 Budapest time. After that, reviewers and the AC discuss privately (Nov 19 → Dec 16) and we can no longer post. Decisions come on Dec 16.

Rules that matter (ICLR 2027 author guide)

  • Revised PDF allowed until the end of discussion (Nov 18). Main text may grow to 10 pages "to allow for new results/discussions"; appendix unlimited. A pdfdiff against the original is shown.
  • Reviewers/AC are not required to read revisions, and may ignore changes that differ substantially from the original. Always say in the comment what changed and where.
  • Unlimited number of comments, each with a word limit. You can reply to each reviewer several times.
  • Comments are public and must stay anonymous: no names, no SZTAKI/HUN-REN, no identifying links. The anonymous 4open/OSF links are fine.
  • Title, abstract and content may change, if the change is communicated.

"Post early" — what that means in practice

  • Yes, post before all experiments are done. Reviewers have only a few days to read, ask follow-ups and update scores, and many check once. A response that arrives on Nov 18 gets no dialogue.
  • But don't post an empty placeholder. An analysis of ICLR 2024/25 rebuttals found that mid-period responses tend to be the most effective. The first post should already carry substance.
  • Recommended two-wave pattern:
    1. Wave 1 (Nov 12–13): a general comment to all, plus one reply per reviewer. Answer every question we can now: text clarifications, plus the CPU analyses from existing data (bootstrap CIs, ties, residual mass/k, non-oracle thresholds, fusion variants, prompt variants). For each API experiment, state exactly what is running and when results will be posted.
    2. Wave 2 (Nov 15–16): post the API results (high-precision BaseJudge, repeated calls, label order/wording), upload the revised PDF with changes highlighted, and post a short "summary of revisions" comment with a section map.
    3. Nov 17–18: answer follow-ups. Politely ask WpPs/Lma8 whether the concerns are resolved; don't pressure anyone.

Per reviewer

Lma8 — Weak accept (3) · the friendly one

Liked: resolution-bottleneck framing with tie/ROC explanation; Manugram is broader than reusing Ghostbuster (regeneration, science, rephrase, self-recognition checks).
Weaknesses: contribution is a scoring readout, not a new detection mechanism; AUROC unchanged; FusionJudge is an equal-weight average with no calibration or selection rule. MG-AI-Sci threshold rests on 1 human doc. Rephrasing covers one generator only.

Questions to answer one by one:

  1. Was the equal weight pre-specified or tuned? Comparison to a rule that switches LogJudge↔FusionJudge by FPR range? → T-FUS C4
  2. Bootstrap CIs / leave-one-out threshold stability for MG-AI-Sci? → C5
  3. Is the FusionJudge–LogJudge AT-HM gap robust to log-prob jitter (App. V) / repeated calls? → C5 A3
  4. How to present the ICLR flag rate so that it is not read as an LLM-share estimate, e.g. a calibration explanation or bounds? → C9 T-ICLR

Goal: keep at 3 or move up. Every question is answerable mostly from existing data.

WpPs — Weak reject (2) · the swing vote

Liked: simple, practical, no training, uses top-k from commercial APIs; low-FPR focus and tie analysis; Manugram is a valuable resource.
Weaknesses:
  1. Residual mass → ½ needs validation: label-absence rates, residual-mass distribution near the threshold, effect of k and of residual treatment on TPR@1%FPR.
  2. Readout vs. precision/prompt confound: BaseJudge outputs 2 decimals under different instructions. Raise BaseJudge's precision and rerun the same oracle protocol. Report distinct-value counts and tie-group sizes near the threshold.
  3. Oracle thresholds and eval-set backbone selection; the domain-shift experiment does not show that zero-shot detectors are stable when the test distribution changes.

Explicit: "If the authors can address most of them … I will consider raise my overall score." All three items map to concrete experiments; answer each with a table.

xghj — Clear reject (1) · soundness 1, informativeness 1

Liked: low-FPR focus; tie-breaking ablation gives mechanism evidence; benchmark and the AI-polished-human analysis have potential.
Weaknesses:
  1. P("human" token) is not a calibrated P(human authorship); "recovering a finer underlying judgment" overclaims.
  2. Readout not isolated: different instructions and different calls, so the gain may come from instruction effects, quantization or inference noise. Wants higher-precision output, repeated elicitation (averaged), and different label verbalizations.
  3. Fusion not motivated: two [0,1] scores are not a common probability scale. It is better called a heuristic ensemble.
  4. No deployment-level FPR control (oracle thresholds, eval-set backbone selection). The domain-shift zero-shot curves are constant by construction.
  5. Limited novelty (answer-token readout has precedents; fusion is an average). App. V checks agreement at 0.5, not at the low-FPR threshold.

Questions: (i) do gains hold with higher-precision BaseJudge or averaged repeats? (ii) FPR/TPR with independently calibrated thresholds transferred between domains? (iii) sensitivity of near-threshold decisions to label wording, label order and repeated calls?

Strategy: concede the framing points (1, 3) gracefully and fix the text. Answer 2 and 4 with data. On novelty, argue that the contribution is the diagnosis plus the benchmark, not the readout primitive. The AC weighs whether the concerns were addressed, even if xghj does not move.
