Paper Summary:
The paper examines how to read an LLM judge's answer when using LLM-as-judge for AI-text detection. The authors argue that asking the judge to state a probability or label directly is too coarse and produces many ties at a 1% false-alarm rate. They therefore propose LogJudge, which reads the top-k log-probabilities of “human/machine” at a single answer token and computes the expected human-likeness; FusionJudge then averages this score with the stated probability. The authors also build Manugram, covering current generators, scientific abstracts, AI-Scientist abstracts, and rephrased corpora, and validate the methods on four splits and on ICLR abstracts. The main result is that, with the same judge, the log-probability readout leaves AUROC largely unchanged but substantially improves recall at low FPR; FusionJudge is strongest on average and requires no training.

Critical Strengths:
1.1. The problem is well framed, rather than attributing the gain to a stronger judge, the authors identify a resolution bottleneck in stated probabilities near the 1% FPR and use tied values and ROC operating points to explain why LogJudge works. This observation has practical relevance for LLM-as-judge detection. 2. 3.2. Manugram doesn’t just reuse Ghostbuster, it regenerates the machine text, adds scientific abstracts and rephrased corpora, and runs domain-shift, rephrasing, and self-recognition checks. Compared with reporting AUROC only on the old Ghostbuster, this validation is broader and more credible.

Critical Weaknesses:
1.The methodological contribution is more about how to derive a detection score from the model’s output than about proposing a new detection mechanism. AUROC remains almost unchanged, the gains are concentrated at low-FPR thresholds, and FusionJudge is merely an equal-weight average, lacking calibration or a selection rule. 2.MG-AI-Sci contains only 100 human texts, so its 1% threshold is effectively determined by a single document; the AT-HM differences on this small sample should therefore not be overinterpreted. In addition, the rephrasing attack covers only one generator and a limited corpus, so the conclusions have limited generalizability.

Questions For Authors:
Is FusionJudge’s equal-weight average pre-specified, or was it selected on a validation set? Has it been compared with a simple rule that switches between LogJudge and FusionJudge according to the FPR range?
Can the 1% threshold and small-sample AT-HM for MG-AI-Sci be accompanied by bootstrap confidence intervals or leave-one-out threshold-stability results?
Given the log-probability jitter in Appendix V, is the AT-HM gap between FusionJudge and LogJudge robust under repeated calls or resampling?
How can the ICLR abstract flag rate be presented to readers so that it is not mistaken for the share of LLM-written abstracts? Under the label-free setting, can a more intuitive calibration explanation or a sensitivity/upper-and-lower-bound analysis be provided?
