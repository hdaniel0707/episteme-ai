  • What it means: with the paper's call (which reasons), BaseJudge's weakness at 1% FPR is mostly coarse values and ties: more decimals or averaging fixes it. About 60% of the 4-decimal gain survives rounding back to 2 decimals, so the 4-decimal prompt changes the answer itself (more reasoning: on 62% of calls vs ~46%), not only its digits. The 4th digit is still mostly filler (61% end in "7"); it breaks ties (human tie group 29 → 1.5).
  • Per split (4 dec. vs LogJudge): LogJudge ahead on GB-orig (0.805/0.786), MG-GB (0.547/0.475), MG-AI-Sci (0.56/0.48); BaseJudge far ahead on MG-Sci (0.865/0.679), where the paper's BaseJudge already led. MG-AI-Sci has 100 humans: its threshold is one text.
  • Gemma (no reasoning; humans_plus_third): 4 dec. 0.424, no limit 0.505 vs LogJudge 0.512 and A2 0.50–0.52: LogJudge ≈ the best BaseJudge variant.
  • What still holds: LogJudge gets this in one call without reasoning (BaseJudge 4-dec.: ~59 reasoning tokens on average); the matched call shows BaseJudge without reasoning is far behind; fusion adds ~0.09 over either part and stays best per call; gemma agrees.
  • Framing (agreed 2026-11-12): same test-time compute. Abstract, intro, main figure and main results unchanged. (1) The main comparison is conservative: BaseJudge already reasons, LogJudge does not (effort none is required for logprobs). (2) At equal compute (matched call) the stated probability collapses (0.058) and the readout carries the signal. (3) More decimals help luna only with reasoning (more reasoning: 63% vs 49% of calls); gemma with thinking off stays ≤ LogJudge, and switching thinking on does not improve it. (4) Averaging is extra budget: at equal calls FusionJudge is best or tied; the ensemble part is not emphasized in the main text (one sentence).
