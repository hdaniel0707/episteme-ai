1. The question (WpPs-1)
------------------------
"Assigning a neutral value of 0.5 to probability mass outside the top-k window or
unmapped to valid labels needs further justification. Please report label-absence
rates and residual-mass distributions near the decision threshold, and evaluate how
different k values and residual-mass treatments affect TPR@1%FPR."


2. The analysis (CPU, existing data, no new API calls)
------------------------------------------------------
Code:    ../logjudge-manugram/src/logjudge_manugram/compute/residual_mass.py

(All of this is still untracked in the logjudge-manugram repo, not committed.)

Setup: rescore every text of the four core splits from the stored answer-token
windows. Two factors:
  - window size: truncate each window to its top k entries, k in {1,2,3,5,10,20};
  - residual rule, r = 1 - p_h - p_m:
      ours        r -> 1/2      s = p_h + r/2   (the paper's LogJudge)
      to human    r -> human    s = 1 - p_m
      renorm      lambda = 1    s = p_h/(p_h+p_m)
      to machine  r -> machine  s = p_h
With k=20 our rule reproduces the released LogJudge scores exactly.
"Near threshold" = every text at or below the 5th percentile of its split's human
scores (luna: 455 human + 23,646 machine texts).


3. Findings
-----------
a) What the endpoints return.
   - gemma-4-31b: all 20 requested candidates, always.
   - gpt-5.6-luna: 1 candidate for 91.4% of the 36,148 texts, 2 for 8.6%, although
     the capture requests 20. In the near-threshold region it is always a single
     token, "machine". The returned logprobs also sit on a coarse grid
     (-4e-6, -8e-6, -1.1e-5, ...).
   - Off-format answers (neither label returned) never occur, for either judge.

b) Label absence and residual near the threshold.
   - luna: "human" is absent for 100% of near-threshold texts, both classes;
     "machine" is never absent. Residual median 0.04-0.12% (per split, human texts),
     max 0.41%.
   - gemma: "human" is present for every near-threshold human text (one exception
     in MG-AI-Sci); absent for 1-67% of near-threshold machine texts depending on the
     split. Residual <= 3.2e-6.

c) Window size and residual rule (four-split mean AUROC / TPR@1%FPR):

                      gpt-5.6-luna                  gemma-4-31b
   rule           k=1          k=2..20         k=1          k=2          k=20
   ours           .975/.648    .975/.648       .919/.509    .923/.509    .922/.512
   to human       .968/.648    .973/.648       .849/.509    .922/.512    .927/.512
   renorm         .887/.000    .932/.000       .842/.000    .923/.502    .927/.509
   to machine     .893/.000    .934/.000       .912/.000    .919/.501    .920/.509

   - Our rule is insensitive to k (luna identical for all k; gemma .509 -> .512).
   - Rules that keep the residual (ours, to human) give the same luna ROC: with
     p_h = 0 both are increasing in 1 - p_m.
   - Rules that drop it (renorm, to machine) set every near-threshold luna text to
     0, so they tie and TPR@1%FPR is 0 up to 5% FPR, while AUROC stays ~0.93.
     For gemma they fail only at k=1.

d) Consequence for the paper: two statements were wrong.
   - "Both judges use k = 20, the largest window either endpoint returns" (API
     settings): luna returns one or two.
   - "Any confidence weight lambda gives a valid score" (method): true for the range
     [0,1], but lambda = 1 (renormalizing) loses the low-FPR signal.
   Also a memory note: never describe the luna windows as top-20.

Final state of each change:

(1) Method, LogJudge paragraph (after Eq. logjudge_general).
    Old: "Any confidence weight lambda gives a valid score ... The choice
          lambda = P(x) makes the fallback proportional to the missing mass and adds
          no hyperparameter."
    New: "Any confidence weight lambda(x) in [0,1] keeps the score in [0,1] and lets
          it fall back to 1/2 as lambda -> 0. We use lambda(x) = P(x), which adds no
          hyperparameter and counts the mass outside the labels as neutral."
    No appendix reference in the method (quantitative results belong in Results).

(2) Experiments, NEW short paragraph "Residual mass." (label sec:results_residual),
    directly after "Why the readout matters, and why averaging helps" and its figure,
    before the domain-shift paragraph. Kept separate because the readout paragraph
    is about ties and fusion:
    "Counting the mass outside the two labels as neutral, rather than renormalizing
     it away, is what lets LogJudge order the most machine-like texts. For these
     texts human is not among the returned candidates, so renormalizing over the two
     labels sets them all to 0 and drops TPR@1%FPR to 0 for gpt-5.6-luna, whereas
     our rule keeps their order and is barely affected by how many candidates the
     endpoint returns (Appendix residual_mass)."

(3) Appendix "Judge Prompts and API Settings".
    Old: "Both judges use k = 20, the largest window either endpoint returns."
    New: "Both judges request k = 20, the largest window either endpoint allows.
          gemma-4-31b returns all 20 candidates, while the gpt-5.6-luna endpoint
          returns far fewer (one or two in our data; Appendix residual_mass)."

(4) Appendix "LogJudge: General Formulation", after the P(x) >= 0.98 sentence.
    Old: "... truncating to the top-k window introduces very little error."
    New: "Appendix residual_mass shows how the treatment of the small remainder
          affects detection."

(5) NEW appendix "LogJudge: Window Size and Residual Mass" (apx:residual_mass).
    Placed after the two "Same Judge, Two Readouts" appendices and before
    "Embedding Analysis", i.e. in order of first mention in the main text.
    Structure:
      - intro: r = 1 - p_h - p_m is counted as neutral; two questions.
      - Setup: truncation to top k and the four rules, defined once, before results.
      - Window size: the endpoints return different amounts (gemma 20, luna one or
        two in our data), so the luna results use less of the answer distribution
        than LogJudge can read; for gemma the extra candidates move TPR from 0.509
        (k=1) to 0.512 (k=20); "a wider window would give LogJudge more to read, but
        the method does not depend on it."
      - The residual must be kept: near the threshold p_h = 0, texts differ only in
        1 - p_m; keeping r preserves the order, dropping it ties them at 0
        (TPR 0, AUROC 0.93). "This is why LogJudge keeps the residual instead of
        renormalizing over the labels."
      - Figure figs/residual_mass (a: share of texts with "human" returned, by
        flagging FPR; b: luna ROC up to 5% FPR for the four rules).
      - Table tab:residual-mass: the four rules x {k=1, k=20} x both judges.
