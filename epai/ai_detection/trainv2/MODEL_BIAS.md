# Model bias notes: RADAR generalization + Denario generator provenance

Working notes from a 2026-09-11 investigation, kept for the article write-up.
Two separate questions, both about whether a `detector`/`gb_orig`/`mg_den`
result is trustworthy or an artifact: (1) is RADAR's implementation and its
uneven cross-generator performance real, and (2) what generator is actually
behind the `mg_den` (Denario) class.

---

## 1. RADAR implementation — verified correct

`epai/ai_detection/score/add_detector_scores.py` reads `ai_index=0`:
`probs[:, 0]` from a softmax over the RADAR (`TrustSafeAI/RADAR-Vicuna-7B`)
logits, calling that P(AI). Checked against two independent sources:

- The model's actual `config.json` on the Hub has **no `id2label`** — matching
  the script's own comment that the direction can't be verified from the
  artifact alone.
- IBM's official reference snippet (`github.com/IBM/RADAR`):
  ```python
  F.log_softmax(detector(**inputs).logits, -1)[:, 0].exp()  # "Probability of AI-generated texts"
  ```
  Mathematically identical to what the script computes. **Index 0 = AI is
  correct.**

No data-pipeline bug either — checked null coverage of `detector_score_radar`
by class on every split:
- `manugram_gb/sci/den.parquet`: 0% null, every generator.
- `ghostbuster_orig.parquet` (raw): 100% null on `human` — expected, pending
  the server rescore ([[ghostbuster-orig-raw-std-split]]).
- `ghostbuster_orig_stand.parquet`: 0% null on both classes — the reason
  `gb_orig` is temporarily pointed here (`splits.py`): it's the only version
  where human and machine are scored on the *same* text regime, so its AUROC
  is a clean number, not a raw/standardized mismatch artifact.

## 2. The cross-generator pattern is real, not a bug

A scoring bug (wrong index/column/text) would flatten or flip *every*
generator together. It doesn't — AUROC by individual generator:

| split | gemma431b | gpt56luna | gptoss120b | llama3370b |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mg_sci | 0.452 | 0.126 | 0.074 | **0.827** |
| mg_gb | 0.673 | 0.386 | 0.409 | **0.969** |

RADAR is excellent on Llama-3.3-70B and **actively inverted** (below chance)
on gpt56luna and gptoss120b. RADAR was adversarially trained specifically
against Vicuna-7B (a LLaMA fine-tune), so it plausibly transfers along model
lineage: great on another LLaMA-family model, broken on structurally
different families.

This matches independently published results. RAID (arXiv:2405.07940,
already cited in `add_detector_scores.py`'s own docstring) reports RADAR
accuracy @5% FPR by generator class:

| generator class | RAID accuracy |
|---|---|
| open-source chat | 81.6% |
| open-source non-chat | 72.2% |
| closed-source non-chat | 67.7% |
| closed-source chat | **48.3%** (near chance) |

Same shape: strong on one family, collapsing to chance on another.

Checked truncation as a possible confound: on `mg_gb`, 49–64% of documents
exceed RADAR's 512-token cap, but **roughly evenly across human and every
generator** — a real limitation worth flagging, but it doesn't explain the
per-generator inversion (which is generator-specific, not uniform).

## 3. No published number matches `gb_orig` exactly

Checked both source papers directly:

- **Ghostbuster paper** (arXiv:2305.15047, the source of `gb_orig`'s
  human/essay/reuter/wp data + `gpt35t`): never evaluates RADAR. Its
  baselines are Perplexity-only, DetectGPT, GPTZero, RoBERTa (F1: 81.5 /
  57.4 / 93.1 / 98.1 pooled).
- **RADAR paper** (arXiv:2307.03838): never uses the Ghostbuster dataset
  (trains/evals on XSum, SQuAD, WritingPrompts, TOEFL — no essays, no
  Reuters). Its "GPT-3.5-Turbo" number is for detecting text **paraphrased
  by** GPT-3.5-Turbo, not text directly generated by it — a different task.

Closest published reference points:

| source | setting | AUROC / accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| RADAR paper | unseen GPT-3.5-Turbo *paraphrase*, avg over XSum/SQuAD/WP/TOEFL | 0.857 (range 0.763–0.955) |
| RAID | RADAR @5% FPR, closed-source non-chat (closest analog to davinci-era GPT-3.5) | 67.7% |
| RAID | RADAR @5% FPR, closed-source chat | 48.3% |

Our `gb_orig` AUROC (0.925, on `ghostbuster_orig_stand.parquet`) sits above
all of these — plausible, since direct generation is generally easier to
detect than a paraphrase of human text, and Ghostbuster's domains are closer
to RADAR's training-era formal prose than a lot of RAID's domains.

## 4. AUROC vs. oracle TPR@1%FPR disconnect on `gb_orig` — explained, not a bug

`gb_orig`: AUROC 0.925 but oracle TPR@1%FPR only **0.118** — looks
contradictory. Checked the score distribution directly:

- **38.4%** of human rows score **< 0.01** (essentially floored).
- Machine rows: only 0.1% that low; machine p1 = 0.026, p5 = 0.053.

RADAR ranks the two classes well overall (good AUROC), but a huge pileup of
human scores sits at the floor, and the exact 1%-FPR cutoff lands inside that
pileup, right where the bottom slice of the *machine* distribution also
lives — so oracle TPR@1%FPR craters even though ranking is fine. This is a
real property of RADAR's (bimodal, over-confident) softmax outputs, not an
implementation bug — but it means **TPR@1%FPR penalizes RADAR harder than a
smoother-scored weak baseline would be penalized for the same ranking
quality**. This is likely why RADAR looked "worse than the low baselines" by
that one metric despite a perfectly good AUROC.

**Takeaway for the article:** RADAR's numbers are legitimate. The pooled
`mg_gb`/`mg_sci` AUROC hides a real split-personality (great on Llama,
inverted on gpt56luna/gptoss120b) that's worth reporting per-generator rather
than only pooled, both because it's more accurate and because it's a more
interesting finding than "RADAR does badly." The gb_orig AUROC/TPR@1%FPR gap
is a distribution-shape artifact of RADAR's own score calibration, not a
detector or pipeline defect.

## 5. Denario (`mg_den`) generator provenance — unknown, plausibly mixed

Investigated what LLM(s) actually wrote the Denario example papers
(`epai/ai_detection/fetch/fetch_denario.py`, source:
`github.com/AstroPilot-AI/DenarioExamplePapers`), since `mg_den`'s RADAR
AUROC sits at chance (0.523) and a heterogeneous generator population would
be one honest explanation.

**In the papers themselves** (checked several PDFs across categories): the
only model-related text is a joke author-affiliation footnote, identical on
every paper:
> `Denario¹` ... `¹ Anthropic, Gemini & OpenAI servers. Planet Earth.`

No methods/acknowledgments section names an actual model or version, and
this line is not paper-specific — it's the same joke everywhere.

**The example-papers repo README**: no model disclosure; points to the main
`AstroPilot-AI/Denario` repo for the generating code.

**The Denario framework paper** (arXiv:2510.26887, "The Denario project:
Deep knowledge AI agents for scientific discovery"): Denario is an explicitly
multi-provider agent pipeline. Supported models:

- OpenAI: gpt-5, gpt-5-mini, gpt-4o, gpt-4.1, gpt-4.1-mini, o3-mini
- Google: gemini-2.5-pro, gemini-2.5-flash, gemini-2.0-flash
- Anthropic: claude-3-7-sonnet, claude-4-Opus, claude-4.1-Opus, claude-4.5
- Perplexity `sonar-reasoning-pro` for citations

Different pipeline stages can use different models (idea generation:
gemini-2.0-flash fast mode, or gpt-4o + claude-3-7-sonnet in planning mode;
code/analysis: gemini-2.5-pro / gpt-4.1 / o3-mini; reviewing: Claude or an
OpenAI reasoning model). For paper **writing** specifically, the authors
state: *"our choice of models has so far been made based on trial and error
rather than systematic evaluation."* Not fixed, not disclosed per output.

**Checked a specific run's raw logs** (pulled one project's `files_url` off
the paper host, `.../Biology/Project4/idea_generation_output/`):
`LLM_calls.txt` only logs token counts per call (no model identifier);
`idea.log` contains the idea text/critique, no model name anywhere.

**Conclusion:** we do not know, and cannot verify, which model(s) wrote any
given `mg_den` document. It is plausible the generator mix varies paper to
paper or even stage to stage within one paper (ideas from Gemini, writing
from Claude, etc.), but the "Anthropic, Gemini & OpenAI servers" footnote is
a joke about the framework's affiliations in general, applied identically to
every paper — it is *not* evidence that any specific paper used all three.
**For the article: report generator identity as unknown/undisclosed for
`mg_den`, not as "a mix of OpenAI/Google/Anthropic."** That framing is
supported; asserting an actual mix is not.

This directly bears on interpreting RADAR's near-chance `mg_den` AUROC
(0.523): unlike `gb_orig`/`mg_gb`/`mg_sci`, where the machine class is one
well-defined generator, `mg_den` may be a blend across families with no
fixed recipe — a harder, more diffuse target than a single-generator
fingerprint, and consistent with (though not proof of) the per-generator
collapse pattern found in §2.
